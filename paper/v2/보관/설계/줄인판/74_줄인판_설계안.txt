Carbon-aware schedulers move jobs to regions and hours with lower carbon intensity, but without regional capacity limits the jobs pile into the single cleanest region. We propose CAST, a carbon-aware scheduler that never commits future slots: every hour it reassigns the region and start time of all waiting jobs with one linear program. In a one-year simulation over eight regions with 2025 carbon-intensity data, CAST missed no deadline, made no assignment above the regional capacity cap, and cut emissions, counted with average emission factors, by 71.9% on a synthetic workload and 51.3% on an Azure VM trace. On the synthetic workload, a design that fixes start times without seeing other jobs' committed plans started 9.0–17.0% of jobs above the cap unless it re-checked capacity at start time, and CAST emitted 1.2 percentage points less than a design that places each job once on arrival and never re-solves it, at a fixed weight matched in round-trip latency. Run without the cap, the two prior policies that save more than CAST's default setting need 2.2 and 9.9 times the home-region peak concurrency in their busiest region.
